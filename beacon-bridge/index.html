Beacon Bridge

A note for grown-ups  ·  the game is paused

A fox has to cross a canyon before the light goes. Each of the three stone spans has a bite out of it, and it takes two blocks to mend one: a lower one that slots into the notch, then an upper one that caps it off.

Controls — keyboard only, nothing to click

  • ◀▶ Slide the block left and right
  • ▲▼ Turn the block a quarter turn
  • Space Drop the block into the gap
  • Q Open and close this menu
  • H Leave the game and go back to all games

What it builds

  • Mental rotation. Turning a shape in the head and picturing how it will look, before committing. One of the most-studied spatial abilities, and it underpins later geometry, map reading and design.
  • Two-step planning. The next block is always shown in the corner, so the first drop has to be chosen with the second already in mind. That is working memory, not trial and error.
  • Persistence without sting. If the finished span is wrong, both blocks float back up and it is offered again. Nothing is taken away.

Session length

Three spans and six blocks on a 90-second light. The sky darkens as it goes, so the clock is something your child can feel rather than read.

How the difficulty adapts

The game counts how many goes each span takes. An average of one and a third or better nudges it up a step; two or more eases it back down, as does running out of light. One step at a time, and remembered between sittings.

Six steps. Each widens the deck or brings in bigger, less symmetrical blocks, and the last span of every crossing is always one step harder than the other two.
