Beacon Bridge

A note for grown-ups  ·  the game is paused

A fox needs to cross a canyon. Every stone span has a piece missing. Your child turns the floating block in their head, lines it up and drops it into the gap. Whole span, fox walks on. Five spans, one beacon lit.

Controls — keyboard only, nothing to click

  • ◀▶ Slide the block left and right
  • ▲▼ Turn the block a quarter turn
  • Space Drop the block into the gap
  • Q Open and close this menu

What it builds

  • Mental rotation. Picturing how a shape will look once turned, before committing. One of the most-studied spatial abilities, and it underpins later geometry, map reading, engineering and design.
  • Spatial visualisation. Holding a shape and a target hole in mind at once and comparing them without moving anything.
  • Plan first, then act. Dropping is instant, so the thinking happens beforehand. A first-try fit earns a star.
  • Persistence without sting. A wrong block rests on the stone so your child sees why it missed, then floats back up. No run can be failed.

How the difficulty adapts

The game counts how many tries each block takes. Clean crossings bring larger, less symmetrical shapes and more places to line them up; a struggle eases it back down.

After three tries on the same block, a soft glowing outline appears in the gap showing exactly how it should sit — so nobody ever gets stuck.

Session length

One crossing is five spans, usually 60–90 seconds. There is no timer and nothing chases your child, so it is easy to stop after a beacon.
