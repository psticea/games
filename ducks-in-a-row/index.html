Ducks in a Row

A note for grown-ups  ·  the game is paused

A mother duck paddles across a pond at the end of the day, gathering her ducklings into a line behind her. Every duckling she collects is worth more than the last. The catch is that a long line is heavy: she swims a little slower and turns a lot wider, and her own line is the one thing on the pond she cannot swim through. Brush it and the ducklings behind that point scatter — they are not lost, they are back on the water, waiting to be gathered again.

Controls

  • Hold anywhere on the pond and she turns towards your finger. Slide to redirect. She never stops swimming, so there is nothing to press to go.
  • ◀▶ or AD held down turn her, on a keyboard.
  • Space start, and play again
  • Q open and close this menu
  • H back to all games

Session length

One round of 90 seconds, with the time always on screen. It ends on a sunset rather than a buzzer, and playing again is one press.

What your child is practising

  • Planning a route. The best duckling is usually not the nearest one — it is the one that leaves her pointing at open water afterwards.
  • Resisting the nearest reward. Grabbing whatever is closest scatters the line sooner or later, and a child works that out by doing it, not by being told.
  • Judging space and turning circles. How wide she swings depends on how many are following, so the same gap is easy early in a line and impossible late.

This is a game, not educational software. It does not test your child, it does not adapt to them, and it tracks nothing. The one thing it remembers is the longest line made on this device, kept in this browser.

No way to lose

There is no game over, no lives and no failure sound. Scattering the line costs the multiplier and the time it takes to gather them again, and nothing else — the score already earned stays put. The reeds at the pond edge turn her gently back in rather than stopping her.
