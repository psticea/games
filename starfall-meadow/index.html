What this game is

Starfall Meadow is a 60‑second catching game. Star‑sprites are flung in big looping arcs across the sky, and your child runs and hops underneath to catch them before they land.

The trick — and the whole point — is that you cannot catch a star by chasing where it is. You have to read its arc and run to where it is going to be. A breeze bends the arcs, so there is a second variable to read too.

Skills it builds

  • Trajectory prediction. Reading a curved path and extrapolating its end point — the intuitive physics that underpins catching, throwing and every ball sport.
  • Anticipatory timing. Committing to a movement before the target arrives, instead of reacting after it. This is a genuinely different skill from raw reaction speed.
  • Visual‑spatial reasoning. Holding two or three moving arcs in mind at once and planning a route that intercepts them in order.
  • Motor planning & sequencing. Chaining run → hop → dive into one smooth intercept rather than three separate reactions.
  • Sustained selective attention. Tracking the star that matters while ignoring a busy, sparkly background.
  • Growth mindset. Missed stars bloom into flowers and are counted as a reward, not a penalty. There is no "Game Over" screen anywhere in this game.

How the difficulty adapts

The game keeps a running measure of your child's catch accuracy. When they are doing well, stars fly faster, the breeze picks up, and the dotted flight‑path guide fades away. When they struggle, the guide fades back in, arcs slow down, and gentle floaty bubble‑stars appear. The aim is to keep them just at the edge of their ability.

Every star is generated by a solver that checks it is physically reachable from where the previous star landed — so a miss is always a readable mistake, never bad luck.
